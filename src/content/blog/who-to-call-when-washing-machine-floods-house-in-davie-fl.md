## Is this an appliance repair call, a plumbing call, or a restoration call?

It depends on where the failure happened and whether water is still moving. If the washer's supply hose burst and water is still running, you need the valve shut off immediately, which anyone in the house can do; it's not a call-and-wait situation.

If the valve won't turn or the shutoff itself is broken, that's a plumber's job, and it's urgent. If the drain pump failed or the drain hose popped out of the standpipe, that's an appliance repair issue once the puddle is handled. In every case, once standing water has touched drywall, flooring, or carpet pad, that part is restoration work: extraction, moisture mapping, and structural drying, not something a repair tech or a mop handles safely. RestorationXpress and companies like it typically coordinate with whichever trade caused the failure while they handle the water damage side.

A failed drain pump usually discharges water that's already carried detergent residue and lint through the system, which the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) classifies as Category 2, or gray water. That means more than a wet-vac and a fan: porous materials like carpet pad and baseboard often need to come out rather than just dry in place.

## Does a dishwasher or refrigerator ice-maker line leak work the same way?

Mostly yes: shut off the water first, then call for extraction if it's reached flooring or cabinetry, with one key difference in water category. A dishwasher leak behaves almost exactly like a washing machine leak. Shut off the water supply under the sink, then treat standing water on the kitchen floor the same way: it's usually gray water once it's picked up detergent and food residue from the appliance.

A refrigerator ice-maker line is different. That's a clean supply line, so a sudden break is Category 1 water, at least until it sits for a while or touches contaminated flooring. Either way, the fix is the same: find and close the shutoff valve (usually behind the fridge or under a nearby sink), then call for drying if water has gotten under cabinets or flooring, since that's where hidden moisture lingers longest.

## Will homeowners insurance cover washing machine flood damage?

Usually yes, if the failure was sudden, like a burst hose or a cracked drain pump, and usually no if it was a slow leak you didn't notice for weeks. Insurers draw a hard line between sudden, accidental discharge and gradual damage from wear and neglect, and that line decides most claims.

Document the damage with photos before cleanup starts, keep the failed hose or pump if you can, and get a written drying scope from whoever handles the extraction; adjusters ask for exactly that. ## What happens when the restoration crew arrives?

A crew arrives, confirms the water is shut off, and maps how far the water traveled with a moisture meter before anything else happens. They'll check under baseboards, behind cabinet toe-kicks, and into the adjoining room, since laundry room floods in Davie homes often travel further than the visible puddle suggests.

From there: extraction of standing water, removal of any porous material that can't be saved (carpet pad, sometimes baseboard), and placement of air movers and a dehumidifier to dry the structure to the [IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard, typically monitored over several days.
