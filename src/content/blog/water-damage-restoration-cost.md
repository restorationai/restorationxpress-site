In Broward County, most water damage restoration jobs run **$1,200 to $7,000** for a single-room or multi-room loss, with major Category 3 jobs or large-scale extraction climbing past $15,000. The exact number depends on how much square footage got wet, what materials are involved, and how long the water sat before anyone called for help. If you're standing in a Davie living room right now staring at a brown ring on the ceiling from an overflowing AC condensate line, or you just found standing water in the garage after a summer downpour backed up the yard, this guide breaks down what you're actually looking at.

South Florida losses look different from a burst pipe in a northern state. Here, the two biggest drivers of residential water damage are AC condensate line clogs (common in homes near Pine Island Ridge and Forest Ridge where units run nearly year-round) and roof leaks that show up during hurricane season storms. Both can sit undetected in an attic or behind a ceiling for days before a stain appears, which changes the cost math from a same-day pipe burst.

## What Determines Your Water Damage Restoration Cost in Davie?

Three things set your price: the water's category, the square footage affected, and how fast mitigation started. A small Category 1 leak caught within hours costs a fraction of the same leak left to sit for a week.

The [S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org/) classifies water damage into three categories, and your restoration company should tell you which one you have in the first visit:

- **Category 1 (clean water)**: from a supply line, water heater, or AC condensate overflow. Lowest cost, fastest turnaround.
- **Category 2 (gray water)**: from a washing machine overflow, dishwasher failure, or a toilet overflow with no solid waste. Moderate cost; some materials need replacement rather than drying.
- **Category 3 (black water)**: sewage backups, floodwater, or any Category 1/2 water left untreated for 48+ hours (it reclassifies upward as bacteria grows). Highest cost; porous materials like carpet pad, drywall, and insulation are typically removed rather than dried.

Square footage matters just as much. A 150-square-foot bedroom with a slow AC leak costs far less to dry than a 1,200-square-foot first floor after a roof failure during a storm.

## What Else Drives the Cost Up or Down?

Beyond category and square footage, a handful of factors swing the final number. Response time is the biggest lever you actually control: water that sits for 48 hours starts growing mold, which turns a drying job into a [mold remediation](/services/water-damage-restoration/) job on top of it.

Materials affected change the math fast. Carpet and pad usually get pulled and replaced rather than dried. Hardwood flooring, especially original terrazzo-adjacent wood floors common in older Davie homes, sometimes needs specialized drying mats to save it rather than a full replacement. Drywall below the waterline (generally the bottom 12-24 inches) typically gets cut out and replaced even on clean-water losses, because drying it in place is slower and riskier for mold.

Florida Building Code upgrades can also add cost on a ceiling or roof-related loss. If the original material no longer meets current code (insulation R-value, vapor barrier requirements), the repair has to meet today's code, not the one from when the house was built.

Finally, access matters. A second-floor bathroom leak that traveled down into a first-floor ceiling (a common path in two-story homes near Rolling Hills) costs more than a ground-floor leak because crews are drying and repairing in two locations, not one.

## Will Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration Costs in Florida?

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst supply line or a failed water heater, but exclude gradual leaks and damage from flooding, which requires separate flood coverage. Your deductible applies before the policy pays anything.

The category of water matters for your claim, too: a Category 1 loss from a clean source is more straightforward to get approved than a Category 3 loss, which adjusters scrutinize more closely for cause and maintenance history. Florida's [Division of Consumer Services](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers/storm/general-disaster-faqs) publishes plain-language guidance on what storm and water claims typically cover if you want to read the policy language yourself before you call your adjuster.

Documentation is what actually protects your claim. Moisture readings at the start and end of drying, photos of the affected materials before demolition, and a written scope of work give your adjuster what they need to approve the claim without back-and-forth. ## What Happens During Water Damage Restoration (and Where the Cost Comes From)?

Each phase of the job adds a line item to your invoice, and knowing what happens at each step explains why the final number looks the way it does.

1. **Inspection and category classification.** A technician uses a moisture meter and thermal imaging to find how far the water traveled, then classifies the water category per S500.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water is pulled with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment. This step is fast but non-negotiable; every hour water sits raises the final cost.
3. **Drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are set in the affected rooms, usually for 3 to 5 days, with daily moisture monitoring to confirm materials are actually drying, not just feeling dry to the touch.
4. **Removal of unsalvageable materials.** Wet insulation, carpet pad, and drywall below the waterline that won't dry safely are cut out and disposed of.
5. **Reconstruction.** Drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint are put back to match the original finish.
