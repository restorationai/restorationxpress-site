## Who should I call first when flood water is rising in Davie?

Call 911 or Broward County emergency management first only if the water is still rising and poses a safety risk, such as blocking an exit, reaching an electrical panel, or carrying debris with force. Once the water stops rising and it is safe to be on the property, call a [flood damage restoration](/services/flood-damage-restoration/) company to begin extraction before mold and structural damage set in.

Homes near the C-11 canal system in western Davie, including neighborhoods like Rolling Hills and Pine Island Ridge, sit close to drainage canals that can overtop during a slow-moving tropical system or a prolonged summer downpour. If water is approaching from a canal or a flooded street and you are unsure whether it will keep rising, do not wait it out before deciding whether to move valuables or leave.

If flood water has reached an outlet, a panel, or anything plugged in, do not walk into it. Shut off power at the main breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water, or have the utility company or an electrician do it for you.

## Is flood water from outside actually dangerous? (Category 3 black water)

Yes. Water that enters a home from outside the structure, whether from a canal, storm surge, or flooded street, is classified as Category 3 water under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry reference for professional water damage restoration. That classification means it is treated as contaminated, not as a clean water event.

Runoff from streets and canals typically carries fertilizer, road oil, and whatever was sitting in the water's path. Given the ranch and equestrian zoning west of University Drive, canal water in parts of Davie can also carry livestock and agricultural runoff on top of the usual road debris. Porous materials that sat in Category 3 water, carpet, carpet pad, baseboards, and often drywall, generally get removed rather than dried in place, and crews work in gloves and respiratory protection rather than just rubber boots.

## What happens when you call RestorationXpress for flood cleanup?

The call starts with a few questions: where the water is coming from, how deep it is, and whether it is still rising, so the crew arrives with pumps and extraction equipment suited to standing flood water rather than general water-damage gear.

From there, the job typically moves through these steps:

1. **Assessment and documentation.** The waterline and affected areas are photographed before any cleanup starts; this record matters for an insurance claim either way it's decided.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water is pumped and extracted from the structure.
3. **Removal of contaminated materials.** Saturated carpet, pad, and other porous materials that cannot be safely dried are removed.
4. **Drying and treatment.** Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the remaining structure, and affected surfaces get antimicrobial treatment because of the contamination involved.
5. **Written scope.** You get a scope of work describing what was done and what it covers, which you can hand to an adjuster.

## Does homeowners insurance cover flood restoration in Davie?

Usually not on its own. Standard homeowners insurance typically [excludes flood damage](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance), meaning water that rises from outside the home such as a canal overflow, storm surge, or street flooding. That kind of loss is generally only covered under a separate flood insurance policy, including one backed by the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.floodsmart.gov/).

Check your own policy before assuming either way; coverage depends on what you actually purchased and your insurer's terms. If a named storm brought both wind damage and flood water to the same property, those are often two separate claims under two different policies. Our [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) covers how to document wind and flood damage separately so neither claim gets denied for missing evidence.

## What should I do before a restoration crew arrives?

Once it is safe, photograph the waterline on walls and furniture, and note the time you noticed the flooding, before anything gets moved or cleaned up. This protects your claim regardless of which policy ends up paying.

Don't run a fan or air conditioning through the affected area yet if the water is contaminated; that can spread contamination into dry parts of the home. Move salvageable items to a dry area if you can do so safely, and avoid walking through the water more than necessary.
