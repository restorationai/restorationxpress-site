## What should you look for when comparing mold remediation companies?

The license number, the standard they follow, and whether they test before clearing the job matter more than a star rating alone. Ask any company you're considering to show their active Florida license, confirm they follow the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/) for containment and air filtration, and ask whether post-remediation verification testing is included or billed separately.

A legitimate remediation job isolates the affected area with poly sheeting, runs negative air pressure through a HEPA air scrubber, removes and bags contaminated porous material rather than just wiping surfaces, and ends with a visual and often an air-sample check before the containment comes down. If a quote skips containment or verification entirely, that's worth asking about directly.

For background on telling cosmetic discoloration from an active mold problem, see [Black Mold vs Regular Mold](/blog/black-mold-vs-regular-mold/) and [How To Test for Mold in Your Home](/blog/how-to-test-for-mold/).

## Does homeowners insurance cover mold remediation?

Most standard homeowners policies cover mold remediation only when the mold resulted from a sudden, covered loss, such as a burst pipe, not from gradual leaks or long-term humidity. Coverage is also frequently capped at a set dollar amount even when it applies. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) outlines how standard homeowners coverage typically works, and policy language varies by insurer, so checking your specific declarations page is worth the ten minutes it takes.

Mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold), which is part of why insurers expect prompt mitigation after any water event.
