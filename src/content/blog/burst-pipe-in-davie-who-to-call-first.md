If a pipe just let go somewhere in your Davie home, the next ten minutes matter more than anything else that happens this week. Here's exactly what to do, in order, and who to call first.

## The First 60 Seconds: What to Do Right Now

Shut off your home's main water valve immediately. It's usually near the street-side wall, inside a garage, or at a curb box near the meter. Turning it clockwise stops the flow at the source no matter which pipe failed.

If water is pooling near outlets, light fixtures, or your electrical panel, do not walk through it. Cut power to the affected area at the breaker box instead, or call your utility if the panel itself is wet. Electrocution risk is real in flooded rooms with live circuits.

Move anything you can lift out of standing water: rugs, electronics, boxes, furniture legs onto foil or blocks. Photograph the water line and the source area before you touch anything else. Florida homeowners policies generally want this documentation, and you won't remember the details later.

## Who to Call First for a Burst Pipe in Davie

Call a plumber first to physically stop the failure, then call a restoration company within the same hour to start drying. These are two different jobs and most homeowners need both.

A plumber repairs or replaces the broken section, pressure-tests the line, and confirms the water is actually stopped. That's it. A plumber does not have truck-mounted extraction equipment, industrial dehumidifiers, or the training to document a loss for your insurer, and a restoration crew doesn't touch your plumbing. Confusing the two roles is the single biggest delay homeowners cause themselves.

## What Happens in the First 24 Hours

A restoration crew's first visit is about stopping the damage from spreading, not rebuilding yet. Expect extraction, moisture mapping, and equipment placement within hours of the call.

The technician uses a moisture meter to map how far water traveled under flooring and inside walls, then removes standing water with truck-mounted extractors. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in next, positioned to pull moisture out of wet materials following the ANSI/[S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) drying standard. Any drywall, baseboard, or insulation that's saturated past the point of drying gets flagged for removal, usually that same visit, to prevent it from becoming a mold source.

## What Your Insurance Will (and Won't) Cover

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage like a burst pipe, but they exclude damage from a leak you knew about and ignored. The distinction is whether the failure was sudden or gradual, and your adjuster will ask about it directly.

Document the source of the break, the extent of the water, and the drying equipment placed, before any repairs start. A restoration company's job-site photos, moisture readings, and scope of work become the paper trail your claim relies on; see [Triple-I's overview of settling claims after a disaster](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster) for what adjusters typically look for. Check your policy's water-damage sublimit too. Many Florida carriers cap this coverage lower than the dwelling limit, which surprises homeowners mid-claim.
