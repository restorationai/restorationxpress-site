## Who do you call first after a kitchen fire in Davie?

Call 911 first if the fire is still active or anyone needs medical attention. Once the fire department clears the home for reentry, call your insurer to open a claim, then call a fire and smoke restoration company, not a general house cleaner, to handle the soot and odor.

The fire department's job ends once the home is safe to enter and utilities are confirmed stable. ## Why shouldn't you wipe soot off walls with a wet rag or vacuum?

Wiping soot with a wet rag or running a household vacuum smears the residue deeper into paint, drywall, and fabric, turning a surface stain into a set-in one that's harder, and more expensive, to remove.

Dry soot from a protein fire (grease, food on the stove) behaves differently than the visible black soot from a structure fire or a furnace puffback. Protein soot is often nearly invisible on walls and cabinets but carries a strong, lingering odor that clings to everything in the kitchen. Wet it with a rag before it's properly lifted, and it bonds to the surface instead of coming off.

In older ranch-style homes around Rolling Hills and Shenandoah, textured stucco walls and popcorn ceilings are especially prone to trapping that film if the wrong method is tried first. Restoration crews start with dry chemical sponges to lift loose carbon without grinding it in, then move to wet cleaning only on materials that can tolerate it. A household vacuum also lacks the HEPA filtration to trap fine soot particles, so running one just recirculates them into the air you're breathing.

## What does a fire and smoke restoration crew do that you can't do yourself?

A restoration crew assesses which surfaces can be cleaned in place versus which need to be removed, uses dry sponges and HEPA equipment matched to the soot type, then treats lingering odor only after the particulate is gone, something a standard cleaning service isn't equipped or trained to do.

The general sequence looks like this:

1. **Assess and document** the soot type, affected rooms, and odor source, with photos for the insurance file.
2. **Contain the area** with poly sheeting and HVAC vent covers so soot doesn't spread to clean rooms during cleanup.
3. **Dry-clean first**, using chemical sponges on walls, ceilings, and trim before any wet cleaning touches the surface.
4. **HEPA vacuum and wet-clean** washable surfaces; textiles and contents often get cleaned off-site.
5. **Treat odor** with ozone or hydroxyl equipment once particulate is removed, not before, since treating odor over unremoved soot just masks it temporarily.
6. **Final walkthrough** to confirm surfaces and air quality are clear.

## What should you do right now, before the crew arrives?

Leave soot-covered surfaces alone, run the AC on fan-only with the filter pulled to limit particles recirculating through the house, and don't eat or use any food, dishes, or cookware exposed to the smoke until it's been assessed.

Photograph the damage before you touch anything, including cabinets, countertops, and any items you're tempted to toss. Keep windows closed if it's hot and humid outside; pulling in outside moisture on top of smoke residue just adds a second problem. Don't throw away damaged contents until your insurer or the restoration crew has had a chance to document them, since those photos and that inventory are what support your claim.
