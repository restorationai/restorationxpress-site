## When to Call Someone Else Instead

Not every water emergency starts with a restoration company. Here is a quick triage guide:

**Call 911 or Florida Power and Light first** if there is any chance of electrical hazard. Standing water near a breaker panel, outlets, or appliances means you do not enter the space until power is confirmed off.

**Call a licensed plumber first** if the source of water is still flowing and you cannot shut it off at the main. A restoration crew cannot work effectively while water is still entering the structure. Stop the source, then call for cleanup.

**Call your gas utility** if you smell gas anywhere in the building. Leave immediately. Do not flip light switches.

**Call RestorationXpress** once the immediate safety hazard is controlled and the water source is stopped. From that point forward, they coordinate with your insurance company, document the loss, and handle the mitigation and drying.

**Call your insurance company** after the restoration crew has started and you have a written scope or at least a preliminary moisture assessment in hand. **Moisture mapping.** The technician uses a calibrated moisture meter and sometimes a thermal imaging camera to find where water has traveled inside walls, under flooring, and into subfloor materials. Water in South Florida homes moves fast through drywall and under tile adhesive. The visible wet area is rarely the full extent of the loss.

**Category and class assessment.** Not all water is the same. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line. Category 2 (gray water) comes from appliances or HVAC condensate. Category 3 (black water) includes sewage, floodwater, or any water that has been sitting long enough to become contaminated. The category determines the PPE, the cleaning protocol, and what materials can be dried versus what must be removed. The IICRC S500 standard governs all of this.

**Containment and extraction.** The crew sets up containment where needed, then begins water extraction using truck-mounted or portable extractors. Standing water comes out first, then residual moisture from carpet, pad, and flooring.

**Drying equipment placement.** LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers go in based on the moisture map. The goal is to bring affected materials down to pre-loss moisture levels, typically measured in wood moisture content and relative humidity, within the drying standard window.

**Documentation for your insurer.** Every moisture reading, every photo, every equipment placement log becomes part of the claim file. RestorationXpress provides this documentation in a format adjusters recognize.

## What About Insurance Coverage?

Most standard Florida homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed appliance hose. They generally do not cover gradual leaks, flooding from outside the structure (that requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance)), or damage the insurer can argue resulted from deferred maintenance.

The distinction matters because South Florida gets both types of water loss. A pipe that fails during a storm is typically covered under the homeowners policy. Water that enters through the ground or an overflowing canal is a flood claim. If you are not sure which you have, the restoration crew can help you identify the source and document it correctly for the right claim type.

One thing that consistently protects claims: starting mitigation promptly. Most policies include a duty-to-mitigate clause. If you wait several days before calling for help and the damage worsens, the insurer may dispute the portion of the loss that resulted from the delay. Calling RestorationXpress quickly is not just about stopping the damage. It is also about protecting your coverage.
