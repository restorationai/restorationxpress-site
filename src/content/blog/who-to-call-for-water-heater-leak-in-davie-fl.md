Separately, call a plumber to repair or replace the tank itself. One company stops the water from going further into your house; the other stops it from leaking in the first place. Most homeowners need both, and the order matters.

## Is this a plumber's job or a restoration company's job?

It's usually both, and they don't compete, they split the work. A plumber diagnoses and fixes the tank: a failed T&P valve, a corroded tank seam, a cracked drain valve, or a unit simply past its 8-12 year service life. A water damage crew handles everything the water touched once it left the tank, drying the slab, pulling wet baseboard, checking the wall cavity behind it for standing moisture.

In Davie, water heaters usually sit in a garage, a utility closet, or a condo closet tucked near living space. On slab-on-grade construction, there's no crawlspace to catch a leak and no subfloor to absorb it slowly, water spreads across the tile or finds the nearest threshold into the hallway or bedroom closet. Homes in Pine Island Ridge and Shenandoah with garage-mounted heaters often see water travel under the drywall plate before anyone notices a puddle, which is why the restoration side of this isn't optional once a tank has actually failed rather than just dripped.

If you're not sure yet whether this is a slow drip or a real failure, our broader guide on [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) walks through how to tell the difference.

## What should you do in the first 10 minutes?

Shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater, then kill power at the breaker (electric units) or turn the gas valve to "off" (gas units), before you call anyone. A failed tank with the water supply still running can empty 40 to 50 gallons in minutes, and a damaged electric heating element sitting in standing water is a shock hazard, not just a mess.

Once the supply is off, do not mop and wait to see how bad it is. Standing water on tile or carpet pad starts wicking into drywall and cabinet bases within the first hour, and the window to prevent mold growth in wet building materials is roughly 24 to 48 hours. That's the real reason the restoration call happens now instead of after the plumber finishes the repair days later, drying equipment has to go in before that window closes, not after.

If the unit sits inside a condo utility closet or a second-floor laundry area, check the ceiling or wall below it immediately. Water moves downward through electrical boxes and light fixtures faster than it shows up as a visible stain.

## What happens when a water damage crew arrives?

The technician's first move is a moisture assessment, not a demo plan. They use a moisture meter and sometimes a thermal camera to map exactly how far the water traveled behind baseboards, under flooring, and into the wall cavity, because water heaters are almost always against an exterior or garage wall, so the drywall and insulation behind the unit are the first thing to check even if the floor looks mostly dry.

From there, extraction equipment pulls standing water off hard flooring or out of carpet pad, and air movers plus a dehumidifier go in to dry the structure to a documented standard, the kind of drying referenced in the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) that the industry uses to confirm a structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch. Baseboard and lower drywall sections that stayed wet too long usually get cut out and replaced rather than dried in place, since saturated drywall rarely dries clean.

## How much water, and what does cleanup usually run?

A fully failed residential tank typically holds and releases 40 to 50 gallons, which sounds small until it's sitting across a tile floor with nowhere to drain. A contained spill limited to the utility closet and an adjacent few feet of flooring is a smaller job than a release that traveled under a shared wall into a bedroom or along a hallway toward Rolling Hills or Forest Ridge-style open floor plans common in western Davie homes.

Cleanup cost depends on how far the water traveled and whether drywall, baseboard, or flooring needs replacing versus just drying. A contained, caught-early leak is a smaller job than one that sat overnight. Every loss is different, and a written scope before work begins is the only way to know your actual number, not a quoted range.

Most homeowners' insurance treats a sudden tank failure differently from a slow leak nobody noticed, which affects what gets covered. It's worth confirming that distinction with your adjuster before repairs start.

Once the water is confirmed stopped and the plumber has a repair or replacement scheduled, getting drying equipment on site the same day is what actually limits the damage.
