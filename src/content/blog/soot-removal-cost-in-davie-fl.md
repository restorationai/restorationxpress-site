## What Drives the Price of Soot and Smoke Cleanup?

The biggest cost driver is the type of smoke residue, not square footage alone. Protein and plastic fires leave an oily, acidic film that's expensive to remove; dry wood and paper fires leave a powdery residue that cleans up faster and cheaper.

- **Fire source.** Grease and plastic fires produce "wet smoke," a sticky, smeared residue that penetrates grout, metal, and porous drywall. Wood and paper fires produce "dry smoke," a powdery film that chemical sponges lift more easily.
- **How long it sat.** Soot is acidic. The longer it sits on metal fixtures, grout, or unfinished wood, the more it etches and discolors those surfaces, which can push a job from cleaning into replacement.
- **Material porosity.** Painted drywall, sealed cabinets, and tile clean up far cheaper than unsealed wood, carpet, or insulation, which often get removed rather than cleaned. When framing or insulation needs to come out and go back in, the work shifts into [fire damage restoration](/services/fire-damage-restoration/) or [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) rather than surface cleaning.
- **HVAC involvement.** If the air handler ran even briefly during the fire, soot gets pulled into the ductwork and keeps recirculating until the system is cleaned, which is priced as its own line item.
- **Contents.** Furniture, clothing, and electronics usually get cleaned offsite and billed separately from the structure itself.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Soot Removal in Florida?

Most standard Florida homeowners policies cover soot and smoke damage from a sudden, accidental fire, since fire is a named covered peril. You'll typically pay your deductible, and the insurer covers the documented cleanup cost above that, per the [Insurance Information Institute's overview of covered disasters](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance).

Adjusters pay based on a written, itemized scope that separates cleaning, sealing, contents, and odor treatment, so photograph every affected room before cleanup starts. Coverage specifics vary by carrier and policy, so confirm details with your adjuster. If you have a dispute with an insurer operating in Florida, the [Florida Department of Financial Services Consumer Services division](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers) handles complaints and questions about carriers.

## What Does the Soot Removal Process Actually Involve?

Professional soot removal follows the [ANSI/IICRC S700 fire restoration standard](https://iicrc.org/s700/): inspect and classify the residue, contain the area, then clean with dry or wet methods matched to the soot type before sealing and deodorizing.

1. **Inspection and pre-testing.** A technician test-cleans a hidden spot to confirm whether the soot is dry or oily before committing to a method.
2. **Containment.** Plastic sheeting isolates the affected area so soot doesn't spread to clean rooms during cleaning.
3. **Dry cleaning.** Chemical sponges lift dry, powdery soot off walls, ceilings, and trim without smearing it deeper.
4. **Wet cleaning.** Degreasing agents break down oily, protein-based residue on surfaces that can handle moisture.
5. **Sealing.** Surfaces that can't be fully stripped of odor and stain, like exposed framing, get an odor-blocking primer before repaint.
6. **Contents and HVAC.** Salvageable furniture and textiles go offsite for ultrasonic or ozone cleaning while ducts get vacuumed and scrubbed separately.
7. **Odor treatment and clearance.** Thermal fogging or hydroxyl generators neutralize smoke odor at the molecular level, then a final walkthrough confirms no residual smell or visible residue.

Call a professional as soon as the fire department clears the property, because soot is acidic and keeps damaging metal, grout, and unsealed surfaces the longer it sits.
