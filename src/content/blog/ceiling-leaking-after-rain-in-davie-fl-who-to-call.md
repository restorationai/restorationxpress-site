## Roofer or restoration company: who handles what?

A roofer stops the leak at the source; a restoration company dries out and repairs everything the water touched after it got in. Hire both, in that order, if the roof itself has a hole, a lifted shingle, or failed flashing.

The roofer's scope is the shingles, underlayment, and flashing: finding the actual breach and sealing or patching it so rain stops coming in. [Water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) covers what happens inside the house: extracting standing water, pulling out saturated insulation, cutting out drywall that's past the point of drying, and bringing moisture levels back down to the [IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard so the framing and insulation don't sit wet. If the roof deck or structure itself is damaged from wind or storm impact, that falls under [storm damage restoration](/services/storm-damage-restoration/) as well.

## What should I do in the first 30 minutes?

Cut power to any light fixture, fan, or outlet below the wet ceiling at the breaker, catch the water in a bucket, and move anything valuable out from under the stain before you call anyone.

- **Kill the power first.** If water is pooling near or dripping from a ceiling light, fan, or outlet, shut off that circuit at the breaker. If you're not sure which one, cut the main. Water and live wiring don't mix.
- **Relieve the bulge, don't ignore it.** A visibly sagging, water-filled ceiling can hold a surprising amount of weight. A small, controlled hole punched with a screwdriver (away from any wiring) lets water drain into a bucket instead of collapsing on its own later.
- **Move what's underneath.** Furniture, electronics, rugs, anything that can be pulled out from under the wet spot should be, before it becomes a second claim.
- **Photograph everything** before you move items or mop up: the stain, the drip, any standing water. These photos are what your adjuster will ask for first.
- **Leave the roof alone in the storm.** If you can see daylight or a wet spot forming on the deck from inside, tarping is a roofer's job, not something to climb up for while it's still raining.

## What happens when you call a water damage crew?

A technician uses a moisture meter and, often, thermal imaging to map exactly how far the water traveled through the ceiling, insulation, and framing, then removes only what's actually saturated and sets drying equipment for the rest.

When a restoration company responds to a Davie ceiling leak, the first hour on site is mostly measurement, not demolition. The crew checks drywall, insulation, and any exposed framing with a moisture meter to mark the true wet boundary, since South Florida's humidity makes it easy to underestimate how far moisture has spread. Insulation that's wet almost always has to come out rather than be dried in place; drywall gets cut back to a clean line above the waterline rather than the whole ceiling coming down. From there, air movers and a dehumidifier run for several days, with daily moisture readings until the framing reads dry, not just the surface. Before anything is removed, the crew documents it with photos, which becomes the backbone of your insurance file. ## Will insurance cover the ceiling damage?

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden water damage from a storm-caused opening, like wind-driven rain through a broken shingle or a tree limb that punched a hole, but they typically exclude damage from a leak that's been dripping for months because of a worn-out roof. The distinction your insurer cares about is sudden and accidental versus gradual and poorly maintained.

That's why the timeline matters: if this is the first time you've noticed the stain and it followed a specific storm, say so clearly in your claim. If you'd noticed a small brown ring months ago and ignored it, be upfront about that too; adjusters can usually tell the difference from the moisture readings and the condition of the insulation above it. If you ever have a dispute with how a claim is being handled, Florida's Department of Financial Services keeps a [consumer complaint and insurance help line](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers) for exactly that.
