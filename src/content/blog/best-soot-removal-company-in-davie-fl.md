## How Do You Choose a Soot Removal Company in Davie?

Choose a company that puts the scope of work in writing before any cleaning starts, not after. A verbal "we'll take care of it" is not a scope.

Ask three questions before you sign anything: Will I get an itemized list of rooms, surfaces and contents being cleaned? Does your crew handle both the structure and my belongings, or do you subcontract one of those out? And who talks to my insurance adjuster, you or me?

A company that hedges on any of those is telling you something. Soot jobs go wrong when nobody owns the full scope, and the homeowner ends up coordinating three separate vendors while still smelling smoke two weeks later.

## What Should a Soot Removal Scope Include?

A complete soot removal scope covers walls, ceilings, HVAC ductwork, and personal contents, and it follows the ANSI/[IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/) for how dry versus wet soot gets cleaned differently.

Dry, powdery soot (typical of a fast-burning paper or wood fire) is vacuumed with a HEPA filter and dry-sponged before any wet cleaning touches it, because wiping dry soot with a damp rag just smears it deeper into porous surfaces. Wet, oily soot (common with plastics, synthetics, or a smoldering fire) needs a different chemistry entirely. This is part of why we tell people in [How Professionals Remove Smoke Odor (and Why DIY Usually Fails)](/blog/smoke-odor-removal-techniques/) not to reach for an all-purpose cleaner first.

The scope should also name what happens to your HVAC system. Soot gets pulled through return vents and redistributed every time the air handler cycles, so duct cleaning or at minimum filter replacement belongs on the written estimate, not as a surprise add-on. Contents (furniture, electronics, clothing, photos) should be inventoried, with a clear answer on whether they're cleaned on-site, packed out to a facility, or assessed as a loss.

## Does Insurance Cover Soot and Smoke Damage Cleanup?

Most homeowners policies cover soot and smoke damage cleanup when the fire itself was a sudden, covered event, which the vast majority of accidental house fires are. Check your specific policy, since coverage details and deductibles vary by insurer.

The part that trips people up isn't whether it's covered, it's proving the extent of it. Adjusters work from documentation: photos of every affected surface, moisture and soot readings, and an itemized scope that matches what was actually done. A company that photographs the damage before touching anything and keeps you a copy of the final invoice is protecting your claim, not just doing the job.
