## When do you need a mold remediation company instead of a handyman or cleaning service?

Call a remediation company, not a general cleaner, once the affected area is bigger than roughly 10 square feet, the mold keeps returning after you clean it, or it followed a leak that sat for more than a day or two. The [EPA's own guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) treats anything under about a square yard as a job a homeowner can usually handle with gloves and household cleaner; anything larger needs containment, negative air pressure, and personal protective equipment a standard cleaning crew doesn't carry.

## What should you do before the remediation crew arrives?

Before anyone shows up, avoid disturbing the mold, keep the room closed off, and switch off the HVAC system so spores don't travel through the ductwork to other rooms. Photograph the area for your records, and keep anyone with asthma or allergies out of the space until it's contained.

Once a technician is on site, expect a moisture meter reading to find how far the water has traveled behind walls or under flooring, followed by poly sheeting to seal off the work area. From there, a HEPA air scrubber runs continuously while affected materials are removed or treated with an antimicrobial, following the process laid out in the [ANSI/IICRC S520 standard](https://iicrc.org/s520/) for professional mold remediation. If the mold is tied to a specific leak, like an AC condensate line, that source gets addressed too; our [AC leak water damage guide](/blog/who-to-call-for-ac-leak-water-damage-in-davie-fl/) covers that scenario in more detail.

## Will homeowners insurance pay for mold remediation?

Most homeowners policies only cover mold removal when it results from a sudden, covered water loss, like a burst supply line, not from a slow leak, condensation, or long-term humidity. According to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance), coverage for mold is often limited or capped even when the underlying water damage is covered, so it's worth checking your policy's specific mold endorsement.

Documenting the damage with photos and keeping receipts protects your claim either way, since homeowners generally have a duty to mitigate damage once it's discovered. Our [water damage insurance guide](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) explains how that mitigation duty works in practice.

## Is the leak or the mold the real problem, and who fixes which part?

A plumber or AC technician stops the water source; a remediation company dries the structure and removes the mold itself. If the leak is still active, water will keep feeding mold growth no matter how thoroughly a room gets cleaned, so the source has to be shut off first, either by you or by a trade professional.

Once the source is handled, the remediation side focuses on moisture levels in the surrounding materials, not just the visible stain. That's why a crew that's done both jobs together, cutting out affected drywall, drying the cavity behind it, and verifying moisture readings before closing the wall back up, tends to catch what a cleaning-only approach misses.
