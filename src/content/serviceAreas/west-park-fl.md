## Restoration emergencies common in West Park

West Park sits low and flat in Broward County, which means afternoon thunderstorms and tropical systems don't drain quickly. Older slab-on-grade homes built in the 1950s through 1970s often have little elevation above the street, so heavy rain events push water back toward foundations and under doors before it can run off. Add South Florida's humidity and you get a second problem right behind the first: materials that stay wet for more than a day or two start supporting mold growth, often inside wall cavities where nobody notices until the smell shows up. Hurricane season brings its own risks, from wind-driven rain intrusion around older roofs to the occasional downed tree or storm surge backup in low-lying yards.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in West Park

Much of West Park's housing dates to the postwar decades, built as single-story concrete block (CBS) construction with slab-on-grade foundations, which is standard across this part of Broward County. That construction type holds up well structurally but means water intrusion has nowhere to go but sideways into drywall and baseboards, since there's no crawlspace or basement to drain into. Plumbing in homes from that era is a mix of original cast iron drain lines and copper or galvanized supply lines, both of which are well past their expected service life in many properties and prone to pinhole leaks.

Broward County sits in Florida's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, so any structural repair, window replacement, or rebuild after storm damage typically falls under the Florida Building Code's HVHZ provisions and requires permitting through Broward County or the City of West Park's building department depending on scope. Flat terrain and a high water table also mean standing water around foundations drains slowly, which is worth factoring into any drying timeline.
