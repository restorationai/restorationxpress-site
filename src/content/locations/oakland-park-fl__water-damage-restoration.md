## Why Oakland Park Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of the housing stock in and around Oakland Park dates back to the 1950s and 60s, when slab-on-grade construction and terrazzo flooring were standard. Those slabs sit close to a water table that rises and falls with South Florida's wet season, and during the heavy afternoon thunderstorms that roll through from June through November, ground saturation pushes water up against foundations faster than it can be pumped or drained away. Add in aging cast-iron and galvanized plumbing in homes from that era, and you get a steady stream of calls that start as a slow leak under a kitchen sink and end as warped flooring and a musty smell that won't go away. Low-rise condo and townhome communities, common throughout this part of Broward County, bring a second pattern: shared walls and stacked plumbing mean a leak on one floor often shows up as a ceiling stain two units away, which complicates both the drying plan and who pays for it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Oakland Park

The first visit is about finding every place water traveled, not just where it's visible. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to check behind baseboards, under tile, and inside wall cavities, because in a slab home, water can wick sideways under flooring long before it shows up as a stain. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable units depending on how the property is laid out, followed by structural drying equipment placed and monitored daily. Terrazzo and tile floors common in older Oakland Park homes hold moisture differently than carpet or laminate: the surface can read dry to the touch while the subfloor underneath is still saturated, so we track moisture content over several days rather than pulling equipment the moment a floor looks dry. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, with documentation at each stage so there's a clear record of when the structure actually reached dry standard, not just when it looked dry.

## Oakland Park Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most water damage claims in this area move through homeowners or condo association policies, and the two don't always line up neatly. In condo and townhome communities, the association's master policy typically covers the building structure while the owner's policy covers interior finishes and contents, which means a single leak can trigger two separate claims with two different adjusters. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings that both sides can use, and where a property is part of an HOA or condo association, we coordinate scheduling around any board notification requirements so work doesn't stall waiting on paperwork. Having that documentation organized early tends to be the difference between a claim that moves in weeks and one that drags into months.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Oakland Park: a lot of the canal and drainage infrastructure in this part of Broward County was designed for a slower, steadier rain pattern than the intense, short-duration storms South Florida sees now. When a storm drops a heavy amount of rain in under an hour, street-level flooding can back up into low-lying yards and garages faster than the swale system can carry it off, even on properties that have never flooded before. If your home sits lower than the street or near a drainage swale, it's worth having a moisture check after any heavy summer storm, not just after an obvious flood.
