## Why Country Club Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Miami-Dade's housing stock, including homes in and around Country Club, was built on slab foundations with little to no crawlspace, which means water from a failed supply line or appliance has nowhere to go but sideways under cabinets, tile, and carpet pad. Add in a summer rainy season that regularly delivers fast, heavy downpours and a water table that sits close to the surface for much of the year, and you get a pattern where yard flooding backs up against slab edges and finds its way in through door thresholds and expansion joints. Older homes in the area, like many built through the 1970s and 80s across Miami-Dade, often still run original cast iron or galvanized supply lines, and those pipes corrode from the inside long before a homeowner sees a visible leak.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Country Club

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off tile, terrazzo, or carpet with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is involved. From there we check moisture content in drywall, baseboards, and subflooring with meters, because slab construction can trap water under flooring long after the surface looks dry. Structural drying comes next, using air movers and dehumidifiers set up to pull moisture out of concrete and framing members at a rate that follows IICRC S500 drying guidelines rather than guesswork. Humidity readings get logged daily so we know when the structure has actually reached a safe moisture level, not just when it looks dry.

## Local note

A lot of homes in this part of Miami-Dade still have original terrazzo flooring under later tile or carpet installations, and terrazzo behaves differently under water intrusion than modern flooring: the slab underneath can hold moisture for days even when the terrazzo surface reads dry, which is why we always check subfloor moisture with a meter rather than relying on a visual check alone. Skipping that step is how mold ends up growing under flooring weeks after a homeowner thought the job was finished.
