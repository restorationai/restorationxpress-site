## Why Roosevelt Gardens Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in this stretch of South Florida were built on concrete slab foundations with CBS (concrete block stucco) exterior walls, a construction style that became standard across Broward County from the 1960s onward. Slab construction has no crawl space to catch or vent moisture, so when a pipe fails or a roof leaks, water has nowhere to go but laterally, under cabinetry, through tile grout lines, and into the wall cavity above the baseboard. Add South Florida's humidity, which regularly sits above 70 percent for months at a time, and you get conditions where damp drywall and wet insulation can start supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours if nothing is done. Summer thunderstorm season brings a second pattern: sudden downpours that overwhelm aging roof flashing and window seals on homes that have gone decades without a full roof replacement.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Roosevelt Gardens

The first step on a slab-foundation home is always moisture mapping, since water trapped against concrete reads differently on a meter than water sitting in a wood subfloor. We pull standing water with truck-mounted extraction, then check behind baseboards and under tile with thermal imaging and penetrating moisture meters, because slab moisture tends to wick upward into drywall long after the surface looks dry. Structural drying in CBS homes generally means positioning air movers to push conditioned air across the slab rather than just drying the air in the room, since concrete holds moisture longer than wood framing. We track drying progress daily and document readings for the insurance file, following the drying benchmarks laid out in the IICRC S500 standard so the home isn't declared dry prematurely.

## Local note

One detail that catches first-time callers off guard: in CBS block construction, water that enters through a wall penetration (a cable line, an old AC line set, a hose bib) can travel inside the block cells themselves before it ever shows up as a stain on drywall. A meter reading on the surface can look dry while the block core is still saturated, which is why we check wall cavities at multiple heights rather than relying on a single reading near the floor. Skipping that step is a common reason mold shows up behind a wall weeks after a homeowner thought the job was finished.
