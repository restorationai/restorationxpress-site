## Why Boulevard Gardens Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in this part of Broward County sit on concrete slab foundations with the water table close to the surface for much of the year. When a supply line fails or heavy rain overwhelms a roof or window seal, water doesn't just pool, it migrates sideways through the slab and into wall cavities before anyone notices a stain. Add in central Florida's long hurricane season and the daily afternoon thunderstorms that build up June through September, and you get a steady stream of calls tied to wind-driven rain intrusion, AC condensate line backups, and water heater failures in homes that were never built with a lot of drainage margin to spare.

Older homes in the area, many built with block construction and original cast iron or galvanized plumbing, are especially prone to slow leaks that go undetected behind tile or built-in cabinetry until the damage is extensive. Newer construction isn't immune either: tile-over-slab flooring traps moisture underneath, which means a leak that looks minor on the surface can be saturating the subfloor and adhesive layer for days.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Boulevard Gardens

The first step on any call is stopping the source and assessing how far the water has traveled, which in slab-built homes usually means checking adjoining rooms even if only one area shows visible damage. We extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps, then move to structural drying calibrated to the specific materials involved: tile and concrete release moisture far more slowly than drywall, so drying plans for Boulevard Gardens homes often run longer than a cookie-cutter timeline would suggest.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned to work with, not against, the humidity already in the air outside, and we track moisture content in framing and subfloor daily rather than guessing based on how a surface feels. Materials are dried in place when possible and removed only when saturation and contamination make that the safer call, following IICRC S500 drying standards as the benchmark for when a structure is actually dry, not just dry to the touch.

## Boulevard Gardens Insurance & HOA Coordination

A lot of water losses here fall into a gray area between a homeowner's policy and flood coverage, especially when the source is wind-driven rain versus rising water. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings at each stage so your adjuster has what they need, and we'll walk you through which category your claim is likely to fall under before you're surprised by a denial. For properties inside an HOA or condo association, we coordinate scheduling and any required notifications so the drying equipment and any necessary demolition don't run afoul of community rules while the clock is still running on the damage.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in this part of Broward County: because the water table sits so close to the surface, a slab that reads "dry" at the top can still be pulling moisture upward from below for days after the visible water is gone. We check subslab moisture with penetrating meters rather than relying on surface readings alone, because closing out a job too early here is one of the more common ways a water loss turns into a mold claim a few weeks later.
