## Why Miami Gardens Properties See Water Damage Issues

Miami Gardens sits on the flat, low-lying terrain typical of northern Miami-Dade County, where drainage relies heavily on canals and pump stations rather than natural slope. During the summer rainy season and hurricane months, that combination means water has fewer places to go, and it often backs up toward foundations instead of away from them. A large share of the city's housing stock dates to the 1960s through 1980s, built slab-on-grade with terrazzo or tile flooring directly over concrete. That construction style resists surface staining, which can hide a slow leak for weeks, but once water gets under the slab or into the wall cavity, the humid South Florida air slows drying and gives mold a head start. Roof damage from wind-driven rain and aging cast iron or galvanized plumbing common in homes of that era are two of the most frequent sources we see.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Miami Gardens

We start with moisture mapping to find out how far water has traveled, since slab construction and tile flooring can mask the true extent of saturation. Extraction comes next, using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is standing and whether electrical safety allows equipment inside. From there we set up structural drying equipment calibrated for South Florida's ambient humidity, which runs higher year-round than in drier climates and means dehumidifiers often have to work harder and longer to pull moisture out of concrete, drywall, and cabinetry. We monitor drying progress daily and document readings for the insurance file, since humidity swings in this region can cause materials to read falsely dry if checked only once.
