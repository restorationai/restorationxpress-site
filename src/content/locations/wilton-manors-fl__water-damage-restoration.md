## Why Wilton Manors Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Wilton Manors was built out in the postwar decades, and a large share of that housing stock still sits on slab-on-grade foundations with original cast iron or galvanized supply lines. Those materials corrode from the inside over time, and a pinhole leak under a slab can run for days before a homeowner notices a soft spot in the flooring or a water bill that doesn't match usage. The city's flat terrain and high water table, shaped by the canals and the Middle River running through town, also mean stormwater has nowhere to go during South Florida's summer thunderstorm season. Heavy afternoon rain backs up against low thresholds and garage slabs, and on canal-adjacent lots a king tide or a hard downpour can push groundwater up through expansion joints rather than down.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Wilton Manors

The first visit is about finding where the water actually went, not just where it's visible. On slab homes we check moisture levels at the base of walls and under cabinetry, since water tracks sideways along the slab before it ever shows up as a stain. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water out of tile grout lines, carpet pad, and baseboard cavities. From there, drying is calibrated to the materials involved: terrazzo and tile hold moisture differently than drywall, and South Florida's humidity means air movers and dehumidifiers often need to run longer than they would in a drier climate. We track drying progress daily and document it, following the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying so the numbers in the file match what an adjuster expects to see.
